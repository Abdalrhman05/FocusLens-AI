## 📌 About the Project

**FOCUSLENS** investigates the growing public concern often described as
**"Brain Rot"** and explores whether everyday digital habits---such as
screen time, social media use, and notification load---are statistically
associated with cognitive performance and related wellbeing indicators.

The project combines exploratory data analysis, statistical testing,
visualisation, and a critical assessment of dataset credibility. It is a
**descriptive, correlational secondary analysis**: it does not train
predictive models and does not establish causation.

## 🎯 Research Objectives

-   Examine the relationship between daily screen time and cognitive
    performance.
-   Compare cognitive scores across lower and higher screen-time groups.
-   Explore social media use in relation to smartphone-addiction labels.
-   Investigate digital load, sleep deficit, and reported stress.
-   Evaluate the credibility and limitations of publicly available
    datasets.

## 🧪 Datasets

The analysis used two public datasets:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Dataset                                    Records Main focus
  --------------------- ---------------------------- ---------------------
  Human Cognitive                             80,000 Screen time and
  Performance                                        cognitive performance

  Smartphone Usage and                         7,500 Social media, screen
  Addiction                                          time, sleep,
                                                     notifications, and
                                                     addiction labels
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

A third dataset (120 records) was inspected but was not included in the
statistical tests.

> **Important data-quality note:** Diagnostic checks indicated that
> nearly all numeric variables in the two analysed datasets had
> distributions consistent with independently generated uniform random
> values. The datasets therefore appear to be simulated. Their numerical
> results should be treated as an illustration of a reproducible
> analysis---not as real-world estimates about human behaviour.

## 🛠️ Tools & Methods

**Tools** - Python 3.12 - pandas - NumPy - Matplotlib - SciPy - Jupyter
Notebook

**Methods** - Data cleaning and preparation - Exploratory Data Analysis
(EDA) - Descriptive statistics - Pearson correlation - Welch's
independent-samples t-test - 95% confidence intervals - Cohen's *d* and
effect-size interpretation - Data credibility checks and visualisation

## 📊 Main Analysis Highlights

-   In the Human Cognitive Performance dataset, daily screen time had a
    small negative correlation with cognitive score (**r = −0.199**).
-   The higher screen-time group had a lower mean cognitive score than
    the lower screen-time group (**54.15 vs. 62.14**).
-   The group labelled as addicted reported more daily social-media use
    (**3.70 vs. 2.25 hours**).
-   Digital load showed almost no linear relationship with sleep deficit
    (**r = −0.013**).
-   Average screen time was very similar across the reported stress
    categories.

These are **descriptive results from datasets that appear simulated**.
They should not be interpreted as evidence that screen time causes lower
attention or cognitive performance. The research also reviews published
literature separately; literature findings are not results generated by
this project's datasets.

## 🧍 Personal Reflection

Alongside the data analysis, I documented a personal habit-change
observation: I reduced my own Instagram usage and reflected on the time
and focus I felt I regained for academic projects and everyday
activities.

This is an **informal, self-reported personal observation**, not a
controlled experiment or a measured cognitive assessment. It is included
as personal context, not as scientific proof.

## ▶️ Reproducibility

The analysis was performed in Python 3.12 using the accompanying Jupyter
Notebook.

1.  Clone or download this repository.
2.  Install the required libraries:

``` bash
pip install pandas numpy matplotlib scipy jupyter
```

3.  Open `focuslens_research_v2.ipynb` in Jupyter Notebook or
    JupyterLab.
4.  Update the `data_path` variable in the notebook to the folder
    containing the datasets.
5.  Run the notebook cells in order.

The notebook generates the figures used in the report. Refer to the
paper's reproducibility appendix for further details.

## ⚠️ Limitations

-   The datasets do not directly measure TikTok, Instagram Reels, or
    YouTube Shorts viewing time; total screen time and social-media
    hours are used as proxy indicators.
-   Both analysed datasets appear likely to be simulated, limiting
    real-world interpretation.
-   The design is correlational and cross-sectional; it cannot establish
    cause and effect.
-   The personal habit-change note is anecdotal and was not assessed
    with a cognitive test.
-   The results should not be used to assess any individual's attention,
    health, or behaviour.

## 💡 Key Takeaway

The goal of FOCUSLENS is not to make exaggerated claims about "Brain
Rot." It is to encourage a more thoughtful conversation about digital
habits, attention, and the quality of the data we use to study them.

**Question your data. Understand your habits. Protect your focus.**
